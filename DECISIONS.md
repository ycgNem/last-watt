# Decisions

Every design decision with its reason, newest at the bottom. Format: date, decision, why, what it rules out.

- 2026-10-10: Energy is a per-tick flow with priority tiers and satisfaction, never a static cap. Why: PLAN.md pillar 1; rules out the They Are Billions model.
- 2026-10-10: Four currencies (money, energy, fuel, embers). Why: PLAN.md; a fifth needs an entry here.
- 2026-10-10: Palette: pending the Milestone 0 six-sprite test (Apollo vs Endesga 64). Not Resurrect 64.
- 2026-10-11: The Playwright MCP is for playing and debugging the build by hand; the e2e harness (`screens`, `shots`, `smoke`) stays the definition-of-done check. Why: harness runs repeat and can be re-read, hand sessions can't. Rules out: a milestone passing on hand-taken MCP screenshots alone.
- 2026-10-11 (superseded below, same day): SpriteCook is a secondary art source, only for jobs PixelLab has no tool for (background removal, sheet slicing, 9-slice UI kits). Everything it makes goes through the `STYLE.md` palette snap and contact-sheet check. Why: one look across the game, and its free tier is 40 credits. Rules out: unquantized art from two generators side by side.
- 2026-10-11: The game is named **Last Watt** (was the working title "Candlepower"). Why: the last lit station, written in the game's own unit; a quick search found no game by that name. The folder stays `candlepower`. Rules out: names built on "Keeper", "Ember" or "Lamplight", which already belong to shipped games or a crowded field.
- 2026-10-11: Art sources: **pixel-art-studio** (a vendored project skill; Claude draws every pixel in a Python build script) for placeholders and early art, **PixelLab** for final art, including the palette test. Replaces the SpriteCook entry above. Why: the owner's call. Code-drawn art is good enough while designs are still moving and costs no generations; the palette is a final-look decision. `pixelpipe.py` covers SpriteCook's jobs (background strip, palette lock) locally. Rules out: SpriteCook, and spending PixelLab generations on a sprite whose building or enemy is still changing.
- 2026-10-11 (resolved below, same day): Flagged as possibly derivative: **Arc light**, a power-only turret named Arc that chains lightning, which is Mindustry's Arc (Mindustry is on the not-list); **"the keeper"** for the player, which is Dome Keeper's name for its player in a wave-defense game with gathering between waves (pillar 3 already calls the player an operator). Smaller: **Hulk** is Marvel's. To be renamed or re-derived before Milestone 1 writes them into `src/data`.
- 2026-10-11: The three flags above are renamed, and the tower is re-derived, not just renamed. **Arc light → Floodlight**: instead of chain lightning hopping between targets, light spills from its target onto up to 2 enemies within 1.5 tiles, and the tower's 14.4 kJ/s is split 4 : 2 : 1, with an unused share lost. (The old "3 targets, 50% falloff each hop" delivered 25 kJ/s from 24 kW at efficiency 0.6, which breaks the unit rule.) **Hulk → Pall**: it casts a shadow, taking every Floodlight share after its own, which replaces "absorbs arc chain hops". **The keeper → the gaffer**: the real job title of the person who runs the lights on a film set. Why: CLAUDE.md's derivative rule; the spill and the shadow come from the light fiction, not from electricity. Rules out: chain-lightning towers, and "keeper" anywhere in the game's text.
- 2026-10-11: `DESIGN.md` §6 rewritten. The old wave 1 check counted damage per Moth and ignored that Moths 1.5 s apart share single-target Lamps; a simulation (`design/wave_sim.py`) showed well-placed lamps leaking 7 of 8. Changes: **Lamp firing draw 6 → 12 kW** (6 kJ/s; efficiency unchanged); **spawn spacing set per wave** (2.25 s for wave 1, 1.75 s for wave 2, waves 3–8 from a throughput rule); a throughput check added for every wave; Lamp tier 2 now follows the tier rule (18 kW, 0.60, 10.8 kJ/s), with the upgrade cost written as base × 1.25 × (n − 1); the Hearth is also a node (radius 4); the tempting mistake is now 4 Lamps + a Node with no factory; the Jar lesson moves to wave 5, the earliest a player can afford a Lab, the research and a Jar; Overcharge's flat price is stated as the one place the model gives energy away. Why: the slice's lessons have to hold before Milestone 1 encodes them as pace tests. Changing the Lamp kept the Moth, every wave's health and bounty, and the economy as they were; halving the Moth's health would have changed all three. Rules out: tuning unit stats to fit a map (tune wave 1's spacing, 2.0–2.4 s, instead).
- 2026-10-11: Open, for Milestone 1's map: the **supply ceiling**. Wave 8 needs about 100–120 kW; one Extractor per deposit fuels about 75 kW plus the Hearth's 8. Either deposits take more than one Extractor or the last waves lean on Jars and Overcharge. To be decided when the map is drawn and checked by the bot.
- 2026-10-11: **Milestones 0 and 1 built in one session, at the owner's request** ("get a playable version... you don't have to fully follow the prompt"). The roadmap order is unchanged and nothing past Milestone 1 was built; the Milestone 2 gate is still open. Why: the owner wants to play the concept before deciding where to take it. Rules out: reading this as the gate passed.
- 2026-10-11 (confirmed below, same day): **Palette: Apollo, provisional.** Picked without the owner's choice because the owner asked not to be waited on; it was `STYLE.md`'s lean. Endesga 64 is saved too (`references/palettes/`, and both are named palettes in pixel-art-studio). Every sprite is a build script on `PAL` in `pixel-art/common.py`, so a swap is one line and `npm run art`. Why: art could not start without a palette. Rules out: treating the palette as decided; the owner still picks.
- 2026-10-11: **All slice art is code-drawn with pixel-art-studio** (two art-director agents, 15 sheets, 0 PixelLab generations). Why: the owner asked for that skill; `STYLE.md` keeps PixelLab for settled designs, and nothing in the slice is settled until the gate.
- 2026-10-11: **Engine taken from Sprocket & Sprout: `engine/loop.ts`, `engine/rng.ts`, `ui/font.ts` only** (the font gained atlas padding and four glyphs: × ÷ → ≤). Input, audio, renderer, lighting and the UI kit were written fresh and small for this game; the jsfxr bank, save scaffolding, GitHub Pages workflow and art importers were not brought over yet. Why: the playable slice needed a fraction of S&S's engine, and its lighting/UI files were wired into S&S's sim. Nothing from S&S's `sim/`, `data/`, `app/` or art was read or copied.
- 2026-10-11: **`td-critic` replaces `indie-critic`** (owner's request): a tower-defense specialist whose reference shelf covers lane TD, power/network defense (Creeper World, Mindustry, Factorio, They Are Billions) and hybrids, held to the not-list. `qa-screens`, `art-director` and the new `balance-auditor` were written for this project. Why: the owner wants genre-specific criticism.
- 2026-10-11: **Fuel reaches Burners by adjacency, not by hand.** An Extractor hands each rock to a Burner touching it. Why: hand-carrying a rock every 10 s per Burner is a chore with no decision in it (pillar 3 wants verbs that are decisions); adjacency keeps the spatial factory puzzle until belts in Milestone 3. **Derivative flag:** adjacent hand-off is common genre vocabulary (drills feeding neighbours in Mindustry, direct insertion in Factorio); it is generic enough to keep and is not on the not-list, but it is noted for the critic. Rules out: belts or arms in the slice.
- 2026-10-11: **Derivative flags on the core model (kept, for the critic to weigh).** Proportional slowdown when demand beats supply, and accumulators that charge on surplus and discharge on deficit, are how Factorio's electric network works; Creeper World also runs weapons off a shared energy network; power poles with a coverage radius are They Are Billions' and Mindustry's vocabulary. Kept because pillar 1 is the owner's premise and the not-list already draws the line (no static cap, no ammo, no pollution aggression); what this game adds is player-set priority tiers, health and damage in the same unit as power, and mid-wave verbs. Rules out: copying any of those games' numbers or UI for the grid.
- 2026-10-11: **Bounty = health × 0.15** (was health / 10) and **wave spacing for waves 2–8 tuned by the bot** (`DESIGN.md` §6, "What the real map and the bot said"). Why: on the real map the wave 1 lessons held exactly (A 0 / S 4 / B 7 leaks), but a sensible bot lost by wave 5–6 at any spacing: towers off the bends deliver about two-thirds of nominal and leaks compound because leaked enemies pay nothing. Unit stats unchanged. Rules out: tuning unit stats to fit the map.
- 2026-10-11: **Wave 1 waits for the player**; there is no countdown before it. Why: a new player reads, places and learns before anything moves; call-early is a tempo choice from wave 2 on. Rules out: a timer on the opening. *(The call-early bonus is gone, see the breather below; Space still starts the next wave.)*
- 2026-10-11 (superseded below, same day): **Shades knock out Nodes whose footprint is within 1 tile of them** (in practice: Nodes touching the path) and **never the Hearth**. Why: "within 1 tile" made exact; the Hearth is the station, and knocking it out would end the network, not teach transmission. Consequence: a Node one tile back from the path is safe, which makes the transmission lesson "where you put Nodes", plus "give power a second route" when the far wall needs Nodes that cross the path.
- 2026-10-11: **Targeting runs as its own step between enemies and the grid** (`systems/targeting.ts`), so a tower that will fire this tick asks for its firing draw this tick. Why: the grid needs to know who fires before it allocates; satisfaction is still computed only by the grid.
- 2026-10-11: **Starved buildings stutter** (flicker between the starved and dark tints, on for about `0.25 + 0.7 × satisfaction` of the time), starved beams turn purple and broken, and cut wires go dark. Why: in the first brownout screenshot, Lamps inside the Hearth's light looked powered despite the starved tint; the stutter reads in a thumbnail. Adds to STYLE.md rule 1, doesn't replace it.
- 2026-10-11: **No mid-run saves in the slice.** A run is about 7 minutes; CLAUDE.md asks for save scaffolding from the first milestone, and it is deferred, not dropped (`PROGRESS.md` → Stubbed).

### After the first td-critic review (2026-10-11)

The `td-critic` agent played the slice (headless Chrome, two real-click runs, bot variants) and
did the arithmetic. Verdict: not fun yet, premise not ruled out; the energy model was never under
pressure, one build (about two Lamps per Burner, Lamps at the bends) won with 19 of 20 lights,
and the Floodlight, Beacon, Lab, Jar, Overcharge and the mid-wave verbs did nothing or lost you
the run. The decisions below answer its issues 1–8. Its full report is in this session's chat;
what was left for the owner is in `IDEAS.md`.

- 2026-10-11: **Tiers default to 1 Works (Extractors) · 2 Wall (towers, Beacons) · 3 Labs · 4 Spare** (was 1 towers · 2 Extractors). Why: critic issue 1; buying more Lamps, the most natural move, starved the Extractors, every Burner went cold and the run was lost by wave 5, and the critic lost a real-click run following every hint. Extractors ask 2 kW each, so feeding them first costs the wall almost nothing. The critic proposed a tier 0; reordering the four tiers does the same job without a fifth bar. Starving the factory is still possible as a deliberate burst (raise the wall to tier 1 with T). Rules out: the brownout spiral as the default outcome of buying towers.
- 2026-10-11: **The breather replaces the 45 s timer and the call-early bonus.** Once a wave has fully spawned and the field is clear, the next wave starts 20 s later; Space starts it sooner; nothing pays for calling early. **Derivative flag (critic):** a call-early bonus scaled by the time left is Kingdom Rush's mechanic almost one to one, and it paid 1 money a run anyway. Why: with the timer running from each wave's start, waves always overlapped (1–15 enemies on the field when the next began, 0 s idle per run), so the Jar and Lab, which live on spare power, never had a quiet minute. Rules out: any reward for calling early unless it is re-derived from energy (the critic's suggestion: trade Lab time between waves for bounty).
- 2026-10-11: **Jars are buildable from the start** (60 money); the Jar research project is gone and the Lab keeps Lamp tier 2 only. Why: critic issue 3; the bot without the Lab and Jar ended on 19 lights, with them on 9, and the game's own nudges led the critic into a lost run with 404 kJ unused in Jars. Overcharge is now a verb from wave 1. Rules out: gating the burst verb behind research in the slice.
- 2026-10-11: **Shades cut any Node within 2.5 tiles** (was 1; supersedes the entry above). Placing a Node shows the Shade's reach in red. Why: critic issue 5; a Node one tile back was safe and still powered Lamps across the path, so every bot run had 0 knock-outs and the transmission lesson only existed in a test that put Nodes on the path on purpose. At 2.5, power that crosses or hugs the path is cuttable, and where Nodes go is the decision. Rules out: a Shade that only punishes Nodes nobody builds.
- 2026-10-11: **A Pall takes only 1/4 of a single narrow beam (a Lamp)**; the rest is drunk by its shadow. Floodlight shares and Overcharge are unchanged. Why: critic issue 2; about seven ordinary Lamps killed a Pall, so §6's "the Pall needs burst" was false and the Floodlight and Overcharge had no job. Light-native (the shadow eats a thin beam), not a generic armour stat. Rules out: answering the Pall with more Lamps.
- 2026-10-11: **Waves 7–8 scaled to about 1.5× the kJ** (18 Moth, 9 Shade, 2 Pall; 30 Moth, 9 Shade, 3 Pall) and **spacing for waves 6–8 re-tuned to 1.0 / 0.5 / 0.4 s** with a bot that Overcharges Shades as well as Palls. **Bounty stays at health × 0.15**, against the critic's call to reverse it: × 0.125 loses by wave 6 even with the fixes above. Result: the bot wins with 10 of 20 lights, leaking in waves 2, 3 and 5; one notch tighter (0.9 / 0.45 / 0.35) and a Pall gets through. Why: critic issue 6, the last third was a victory lap with 167–704 money unspent. Rules out: tuning unit stats to fit the map (unchanged rule).
- 2026-10-11: **UI fixes from the review** (critic issues 7–8): the UI goes to 2× from 1000 × 580 and the map zoom fits 26 × 14 tiles, so a laptop tab isn't at 1×; a whole tier is shed only by Shift-click (a stray click shed Fuel); T raises priority one tier instead of cycling 2 → 3 → 4 → 1; networks rebuild while paused, so building before wave 1 no longer shows NO POWER; the brownout ends only above 75% for 2 s (it flapped); the lose screen names lights lost by enemy and the worst wave; tooltips don't draw over the end screen or help; the spiral hint reads the building's actual tier. Why: each was seen in play. Rules out: nothing.
- 2026-10-11: **Derivative flags from the critic, and what was done.** "Satisfaction" (Factorio's own word for its proportional slowdown) is removed from player-facing text ("its share of power"); the model and the internal name stay. The Floodlight's 4 : 2 : 1 spill reads as "the Mindustry Arc minus the arc", a generic bounce tower; **held for the owner**, the critic would re-derive it as a cone every enemy inside shares. **20 lights with big enemies costing more** is Kingdom Rush's 20 hearts; **kept for now, for the owner**, the critic would have leaks drain the Hearth's kW so a leak weakens the grid (pillar 1). Kept as generic: Nodes with a coverage radius (They Are Billions, Mindustry), Shades cutting the network (Creeper World relays), the name Overcharge (StarCraft II, low risk), and wave 1's "inside of the bends" lesson (core lane-TD skill, accepted only as tutorial scaffolding).
- 2026-10-11: **The slice is published on GitHub Pages for outside playtesting** (owner's request, so a friend can play it), from a public repo, deployed by `.github/workflows/deploy.yml` on every push to `main`; the workflow runs `npm test` before it builds, so a red test never ships. The play link lives in the repo's About box, not in a file (CLAUDE.md: no account names in files). Why: the Milestone 2 gate needs players who aren't the author. Rules out: reading a public build as the gate passed.
- 2026-10-11: **Palette: Apollo, confirmed by the owner** after playing the slice. Supersedes the provisional entry above. Why: the owner's call; the code-drawn slice art already reads powered / starved / dark in it. Rules out: the Endesga 64 swap and a separate palette test (the slice served as one).
